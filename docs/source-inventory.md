## Source priority and limitations

1. **Outcome ground truth:** official state-certified results, using FEC compilation for common format; MIT/MEDSL as independently cross-checkable standardized returns.
2. **Individual polls:** original polls, archived FiveThirtyEight rows and their source links; use available Roper records to fill *specific* gaps, not assumed full coverage.
3. **Historical averages:** archive the actual published final average and its cutoff if available; do not describe a newly calculated mean as an original archived polling average.
4. **Methods/context:** AAPOR reviews and original pollster methodology disclosures, with clear provenance.
5. **Licensing:** record publisher and license for *each file* before mirroring or committing; an openly browsable source is not necessarily public-domain or redistributable.

## Not yet verified

- Which election years and geographic units have comparable final-14-day individual polling records (particularly 2000–2008).
- Whether pollster names, sample types, weighting attributes and field dates survive historical exports.
- Direct download/readability of the latest FiveThirtyEight historical CSV, AAPOR replication materials and current Harvard Dataverse presidential file.
- Availability of complete historical *state* averages, original cutoffs and archived methodologies.
- Whether third-party archives' licensing and historical revisions permit repository storage.

## Audit procedure for each candidate file

Record URL, publisher, download timestamp (UTC), file version/hash, data license, election years, contests, geographic identifiers, field-date definition, candidate mapping, sample population, denominator definition, missingness, duplicate rules, number of rows before/after filters and any known revisions. Do not label a dataset usable until its content has been opened and inspected.

## Additional discovery: 2016 Georgia vote-denominator disagreement

The [official 2016 Georgia Secretary of State statewide results PDF](https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/2022-01/november_2016_generalelection_totals.pdf) lists Trump 2,089,104; Clinton 1,877,963; Johnson 125,306, and **4,092,373** as the total across its three *printed candidate rows*. It separately reports **4,165,405 total ballots cast**, which is **not** interchangeable with the presidential candidate vote total. The [2016 Georgia Certificate of Ascertainment](https://www.archives.gov/files/electoral-college/2016/ascertainment-georgia.pdf) reproduces the three electoral slate counts but provides **no write-in/other candidate subtotal**.

Additional independent historical compilations disagree on the comprehensive presidential total: [Dave Leip's Atlas](https://uselectionatlas.org/RESULTS/state.php?fips=13&off=0&year=2016) shows **4,146,825**, apparently including additional write-in categories, while [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Presidential_election_in_Georgia%2C_2016) shows **4,114,732** and attributes its figures to the FEC. The difference is **unresolved**; these are *claims by different compilations*, not interchangeable certified totals. This is a source-audit finding, **not** evidence of election irregularities. Before using a 2016 GA all-valid-presidential-vote denominator, verify the vote-category definitions against an authoritative comprehensive official canvass or complete FEC state table, including write-in handling, and log the selected definition and rejected alternatives.

For 2016 Pennsylvania, the [state Certificate of Ascertainment](https://www.archives.gov/files/electoral-college/2016/ascertainment-pennsylvania.pdf) verifies Trump 2,970,733 and Clinton 2,926,441, plus other named electors, but does not independently establish the complete all-presidential-votes denominator with scattered write-ins. The [FEC 2016 compilation](https://www.fec.gov/documents/1889/federalelections2016.pdf) indexes a total, but its large PDF could not be fully inspected in this session. Keep its complete-denominator field blank until a reproducible official count/total source is confirmed.

## 2026-10-31 Pilot 02 source-validation updates (current)

- **United States House Clerk, [Statistics of the Presidential and Congressional Election, 2016](https://clerk.house.gov/member_info/electionInfo/2016/statistics2016.pdf):** PDF printed p. 16, Georgia: Trump 2,089,104; Clinton 1,877,963; Libertarian 125,306; write-ins 22,359; **total 4,114,732**. PDF printed p. 64, Pennsylvania: Trump 2,970,733; Clinton 2,926,441; Constitution 21,572; Green 49,941; Libertarian 146,715; **total 6,115,402**. Both page images inspected. These official published denominators complete all nine pilot state-years. The earlier 2016 Georgia summary with 4,092,373 listed only three candidates; it did not include the House Clerk's 22,359 write-ins. Dave Leip's Atlas 4,146,825 represents another compilation and **was not used**.
- **FiveThirtyEight [State of the Polls 2024 archive](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/state-of-the-polls-2024):** complete original `2016_polls.csv` (2,677 records), `2020_polls.csv` (2,663), and `2024_polls.csv` (1,700) were acquired through GitHub and committed under `data/raw/fivethirtyeight/state-of-the-polls-2024/`. Source and destination Git blobs matched byte-for-byte. They are metadata for polls ending **15–180 days** before election (2024 publication cutoff Oct. 28) and do **not** contain vote-share results.
- **Additional poll-level source candidate:** FiveThirtyEight's [pollster ratings README](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/master/pollster-ratings/README.md) documents poll and question IDs, candidate vote shares and median field dates. The `pollster-ratings/raw_polls.csv` GitHub blob exists, but its **multi-megabyte contents were not accessible via the available file-read interface**; historical versions from 2016 and 2020 were similarly unextracted. It is not yet independently audited for complete final-14-day coverage or date granularity.
- **Other possibilities:** [FiveThirtyEight 2016 general-model CSV](https://projects.fivethirtyeight.com/general-model/president_general_polls_2016.csv) listed by historical [dslabs R dataset documentation](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/dslabs/html/polls_us_election_2016.html), FiveThirtyEight 2020 forecast dataset links and [Simon Willison's 538 polling mirror](https://github.com/simonw/fivethirtyeight-polls). Links verified; **full vote-share files not acquired in this pilot**.
- **Code and data license:** [FiveThirtyEight's master README](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/master/README.md) states CC BY 4.0 for datasets unless noted. Attribution and exact source blobs documented in the archived metadata README.

**Remaining essential data gap:** comparable final-week **candidate-level polling vote-share files**, including field-end dates, candidate matchup, sample and question identifiers. The official outcome gap is **closed**; the polling measurement gap remains **open**. See [Pilot 02](pilot-02.md).

## 2026-10-31 late Pilot 02 source acquisition (supersedes 2020 access limitation)

A historical third-party mirror of **original FiveThirtyEight presidential poll-answer data** was successfully read in full using the GitHub **blob** API, even though the ordinary large-file read returned no text: [Simon Willison's fivethirtyeight-polls](https://github.com/simonw/fivethirtyeight-polls), `president_polls.csv`, Git blob **`cbe565b7647059de8b58414e420d2b8587250cc6`**, 5,545,063 bytes. The mirror identifies the upstream original FiveThirtyEight dataset and carries a **CC BY 4.0** license. The source file contains **2020-cycle data**, not a 2024 polling release. Its long-format schema contains `question_id`, `poll_id`, `state`, `pollster`, `population`, `sample_size`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `candidate_party`, `pct`, `methodology`, and original poll source URLs.

An exact 2020 final 1–14 days **Biden/Trump paired-question extract** for GA/PA/WI is committed at [data/processed/pilot_2020_final14_paired_questions.csv](../data/processed/pilot_2020_final14_paired_questions.csv), **175 unique question IDs (121 state-specific poll IDs)**. Two Marquette Wisconsin data points (749 LV: 48/43 and 806 RV: 47/43) were verified against original Marquette October 2020 reporting. No complete audit of all other questions has been done. The raw 5.5 MB historical source was **not** committed; the derived file preserves source blob, poll source links, and audit dates. See [source and methodological addendum](pilot-02-poll-data-addendum.md) and [reproducible extraction recipe](../analysis/extract_2020_final14_pairs.py). The script has not yet been executed against the original source locally.

The current FiveThirtyEight pollster-rating `raw_polls.csv` can also be retrieved by GitHub blob API (blob `08735ea503ad64c67cfa285fd75c0a270fdca245`, ~4.96 MB); its election cycle coverage ends in **2023** and does **not** include 2024 general-election outcome rows. A separate 2020 edition of the pollster ratings covers previous general elections including 2016; however its `polldate` is a midpoint/median date and is not an adequate replacement for field-end classification without cross-checking.

**Current gap is narrower:** equivalent **2016 and 2024** final-week candidate poll datasets, more 2020 original-report spot checks, and a frozen rule for selecting one comparable question per poll.
